How to choose a web design agency

The questions that separate a good partner from an expensive mistake, whether you're in El Paso or anywhere else.

Updated November 2026

Talk to us

Hiring a web design agency is a bigger decision than it looks. The right partner shapes how customers see you for years. The wrong one leaves you with a site you can't update, a domain you don't control and a bill for doing it all again. Whether you're comparing studios in El Paso or anywhere else, these are the questions that matter.

1. Can they explain your business back to you?

A good agency asks about your customers, goals and competitors before talking about colors or platforms. If the first conversation is all about their process and none about your business, that's a sign of a template-driven shop.

2. Is the work actually theirs, and actually good?

Ask to see live sites, not just screenshots, and ask what role the agency played on each one. Open them on your phone. Are they fast? Easy to read? Would you trust that business? Many agencies keep some client work private, so ask whether they can share examples on a call.

3. Who will do the work?

In larger agencies, the person who sells the project often isn't the person who builds it. Ask who you'll work with day to day, who designs, who develops and who you call when something breaks.

4. What's included, in writing?

A professional proposal lists the pages, content help, features, rounds of revisions, timeline and price, and separates one-time costs from monthly ones. Be cautious with quotes that are a single number with no detail.

5. Who owns everything at the end?

Your domain, your content and your accounts should be in your organization's name. Ask directly. It's the difference between a partner and a hostage situation.

6. Is speed, accessibility and SEO included?

These shouldn't be upsells. Ask how they test performance and accessibility, and whether page titles, structured data, a sitemap and Search Console setup are part of the build. If they mention AI search, ask what they actually do for it.

7. What happens after launch?

Websites need updates, security, backups and the occasional new page. Ask what support looks like, how fast they respond and what changes cost. If they also handle hosting and business email, you have one place to call instead of four.

8. Do you actually like working with them?

You'll make dozens of decisions together. Clear communication, honest answers and a little warmth matter more than most people expect.

A quick checklist

  • They asked about your business before pitching a solution
  • You've seen live work and know their role on it
  • You know who will do the work
  • The proposal is detailed and in writing
  • You'll own the domain, content and accounts
  • Speed, accessibility and SEO foundations are included
  • There's a clear plan for after launch

Questions

What should I look for in a web design agency?

Look for a partner who understands your business, shows live work, tells you who will do the work, puts scope and price in writing, leaves you owning your domain and content, includes speed, accessibility and SEO foundations, and has a clear plan for support after launch.

Should I hire a local agency or a remote one?

Either can work well. A local agency understands your market and can meet in person; a remote one may offer specialized skills. What matters most is fit, communication and the quality of the work.
