Webflow vs. WordPress vs. custom code

An honest comparison of the three most common ways to build a business website.

Updated November 2026

Talk to us

"Which platform should we use?" is one of the first questions clients ask. The honest answer: it depends on who updates the site, how often, and what it needs to do. Here's how the three most common options compare.

Webflow

A hosted visual platform that lets designers build custom layouts without a template, with a built-in editor for content updates.

  • Best for: marketing sites where your team edits text, images and blog posts regularly.
  • Strengths: polished custom design, a friendly editor, hosting and security handled for you.
  • Trade-offs: ongoing platform fees, less flexibility for complex custom functionality, and your site lives on Webflow's infrastructure.

WordPress

The world's most widely used content management system, open source and endlessly extendable with themes and plugins.

  • Best for: content-heavy sites, blogs and organizations already invested in the WordPress ecosystem.
  • Strengths: huge ecosystem, flexible, many people know how to use it.
  • Trade-offs: plugin-heavy sites can get slow and need regular updates to stay secure. It needs active maintenance, or it ages badly.

Custom code

A site built by hand with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, often as a fast static site, sometimes connected to a headless CMS for editing.

  • Best for: sites where speed, performance and design precision matter most, and content changes are occasional or handled by your agency.
  • Strengths: the fastest possible pages, very little to break or patch, complete design freedom, inexpensive hosting.
  • Trade-offs: without a CMS, content edits usually go through a developer.

How to decide

  • Your team updates content weekly: Webflow, or custom code with a headless CMS.
  • You publish a lot of articles and need plugins: WordPress, properly maintained.
  • Speed and polish matter most, and updates are occasional: custom code.
  • You sell products: usually Shopify, which is a separate conversation.

Our own site is hand-coded and scores 100 across Google Lighthouse. We build in all three approaches and recommend the one that fits how your organization actually works.

Questions

Is Webflow better than WordPress?

Neither is better for everyone. Webflow suits design-led marketing sites your team edits regularly. WordPress suits content-heavy sites that rely on plugins, as long as it's actively maintained.

Is a custom-coded website hard to update?

It depends on the setup. Without a CMS, edits usually go through a developer. With a headless CMS, your team can edit content while keeping the speed of custom code.

Which platform is best for SEO?

Any of them can rank well. Speed, clean structure, good content and correct technical setup matter more than the platform itself.
