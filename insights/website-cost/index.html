What does a website cost?

A clear guide to what drives the investment in a website, and how to compare proposals with confidence.

Updated November 2026

Get a proposal

Ask ten studios what a website costs and you'll hear ten very different numbers. Most of them are honest. "A website" can mean anything from a single landing page to a multilingual platform with e-commerce and integrations. Here is what actually drives the investment, so you can compare proposals fairly.

What sets the price

Scope and templates. The number of unique page layouts matters more than the page count. Ten service pages sharing one template cost far less than ten bespoke layouts.

Strategy and content. Discovery, messaging and copywriting are the most underestimated parts of any project, and often the most valuable. Finished content speeds things up. Content created from scratch is real work and belongs in the proposal.

Brand. If you need a new or refreshed identity, it's best done alongside the website so the two are designed together.

Design: bespoke or template. Templates cost less up front but look like everyone else's and are often slower. Bespoke design is built around your audience and usually performs better in search and conversion.

Features and integrations. E-commerce, booking, payments, member areas, CRMs and custom functionality each add design, development and testing time.

Languages. A bilingual site needs translated content and the right technical setup, so search engines serve each visitor the correct language.

Ongoing services. Hosting, domains, business email, maintenance and SEO are recurring. A good proposal separates one-time build costs from monthly costs.

Costs beyond the build

  • Domain registration, renewed yearly
  • Hosting, from minimal for static sites to monthly platform fees
  • Business email, billed per user on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365
  • Care, support and growth, monthly or as needed

How to compare proposals

  • Check that each covers the same pages, content help, features and services.
  • Ask who owns the domain, content and site at the end.
  • Ask what happens after launch and what changes cost.
  • Ask whether speed, accessibility and SEO foundations are included.
  • Ask who will actually do the work.

How we price

Questions

How much does a website cost?

It depends mainly on scope and templates, strategy and content, brand needs, bespoke versus template design, features and integrations, languages and ongoing services. A written proposal based on your specific needs is the only reliable number.

Is a cheap website worth it?

Sometimes, for a very simple need. But a slow, generic or hard-to-maintain site can cost more in lost customers than it saves. Compare what's included, not only the price.

What are the ongoing costs of a website?

Typically a domain, hosting and business email, plus optional care, support and SEO. The build itself is usually a one-time project cost.
