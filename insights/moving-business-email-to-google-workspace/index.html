Moving your business email to Google Workspace

A calm, step-by-step plan for switching email providers without losing messages.

Updated November 2026

Talk to us

Moving business email sounds risky, and done carelessly it is. Done with a plan, your team barely notices. Here's the approach we use when moving organizations to Google Workspace, from an old host, a free email account or another provider.

Before you start

  • List every mailbox and alias. Include shared addresses like info@ and billing@, and forwarding rules people depend on.
  • Find your domain and DNS access. You'll need to log in to wherever your domain's DNS is managed. This is the step that most often stalls a migration.
  • Pick a quiet switchover window, such as a Friday evening or weekend.

The steps

  1. Set up Google Workspace and verify that you own your domain by adding a record to your DNS.
  2. Create users and aliases to match your existing addresses exactly.
  3. Copy existing email. Google provides migration tools that copy mail from most providers into the new mailboxes. Run the first copy before the switch.
  4. Switch your MX records so new mail starts arriving at Google. Changes can take a few hours to spread across the internet, so keep the old mailboxes active for now.
  5. Run a final copy to catch messages that arrived at the old provider during the switch.
  6. Set up email authentication: SPF, DKIM and DMARC records. They prove your messages are really from you and keep them out of spam. Major providers now expect them.
  7. Connect devices such as phones, Outlook or Apple Mail, and confirm everyone can send and receive.
  8. Keep the old account briefly, then cancel it once everything is confirmed.

Common pitfalls

  • Forgetting aliases and shared inboxes
  • Changing MX records before mailboxes are ready
  • Skipping SPF, DKIM and DMARC, so mail lands in spam
  • Cancelling the old provider too early
  • Domains registered in a former employee's or vendor's personal account

Questions

Will I lose emails when switching to Google Workspace?

Not if the migration is planned. Existing mail is copied before and after the switch, and the old account stays active until everything is confirmed.

How long does an email migration take?

Setup and the switch itself are usually done in a day or two. Copying large mailboxes can run longer in the background.

What are SPF, DKIM and DMARC?

They are DNS records that prove email from your domain is legitimate. They improve deliverability and protect your domain from being impersonated.
