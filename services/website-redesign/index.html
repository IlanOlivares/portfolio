Website redesign that keeps what works and fixes what doesn't

If your site feels dated, is hard to update or simply isn't bringing in inquiries, a redesign can turn it into your hardest-working employee.

Start a project

Signs it's time for a redesign

  • You hesitate to send people to your own website.
  • It looks broken or cramped on phones.
  • Visitors call to ask questions the site should answer.
  • Pages load slowly or the site is hard to update.
  • Your services, branding or audience have changed since it was built.

A redesign that protects your rankings

The biggest risk in a redesign is losing search traffic you've already earned. Before I change anything, I review your current site: which pages bring in visitors, which ones people actually use, and what search engines already know about you. Those pages keep their value through proper redirects, preserved content and careful URL planning.

Clearer message, better results

Most underperforming sites have the same problem: visitors can't quickly tell what you do, whether you serve them or how to reach you. I restructure the content around those questions, modernize the design, and make every call to action easy to find on any device.

Faster and easier to maintain

Older sites often carry years of plugins and patches. A rebuild with clean, modern code loads faster, is more secure and is much easier to keep current, whether you edit it yourself or I handle updates for you.

Common questions

Straight answers about website redesign in El Paso.

Will I lose my Google rankings if I redesign?

Not if it's done carefully. I map your existing pages, keep valuable content and set up redirects so search engines and visitors land in the right place. A faster, clearer site often improves rankings over time.

Can you keep my logo and branding?

Yes. I can work within your existing brand, refresh it lightly or help you modernize it. That's up to you.

My site is on WordPress, Wix or Squarespace. Can you redesign it?

Yes. I'll recommend whether to rebuild on the same platform or move to something faster and easier to maintain, based on what's best for you, not what's easiest for me.

How long does a redesign take?

Usually somewhere from a few weeks to a few months, depending on the size of the site and how quickly content and feedback come together.
